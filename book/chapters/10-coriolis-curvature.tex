\chapter{Coriolis, curvature and map projections}\label{ch:cor}

WRF integrates on a plane that a map projection lays over the curved, rotating Earth. Three kinds of terms account for
this:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the map-scale factors, which appear in every derivative (\cref{ch:eq});
  \item the Coriolis terms of the rotating frame;
  \item the curvature terms of the sphere.
\end{itemize}
This chapter states the last two as \src{dyn_em/module_big_step_utilities_em.F}{coriolis} and
\src{dyn_em/module_big_step_utilities_em.F}{curvature} compute them.

\section{Map-scale factors}

A map projection maps the sphere onto a plane where the grid is regular. The map-scale factor $m$ is the ratio of a
distance on the grid to the corresponding distance on the Earth. WRF keeps two factors, $m_x$ and $m_y$, which are
equal for the conformal projections (Lambert conformal, polar stereographic, Mercator). Each factor is stored at
mass, $u$ and $v$ points: \code{msftx}, \code{msfty}, \code{msfux}, \code{msfuy}, \code{msfvx}, \code{msfvy}. Over the
Eaton domains, which span a few hundred kilometres, $m$ differs from 1 by less than one percent. It still enters every
flux and every tendency, so it must enter them on the GPU exactly as on the CPU.

\section{Coriolis}

With the Coriolis parameter $f = 2\Omega_E\sin\varphi$, its horizontal companion $e = 2\Omega_E\cos\varphi$
($\varphi$ the latitude, $\Omega_E$ the Earth's rotation rate), and the angle $\alpha_r$ between the grid's $y$ axis
and the meridians (\code{sina}, \code{cosa}), the Coriolis terms added to the flux tendencies are
\begin{align}
  F_{U,\mathrm{cor}} &= \frac{m_x}{m_y}\,f\,V \;-\; e\,\cos\alpha_r\,W, \notag\\
  F_{V,\mathrm{cor}} &= -\frac{m_y}{m_x}\,f\,U \;+\; \frac{m_y}{m_x}\,e\,\sin\alpha_r\,W, \label{eq:coriolis}\\
  F_{W,\mathrm{cor}} &= e\,\Bigl(\cos\alpha_r\,U - \frac{m_x}{m_y}\,\sin\alpha_r\,V\Bigr). \notag
\end{align}
Because $U$, $V$ and $W$ live at different points of the staggered grid, each term needs an average of the other
components. For the $U$ equation at a $u$ point, the code writes
\begin{lstlisting}
ru_tend(i,k,j) = ru_tend(i,k,j) + (msfux(i,j)/msfuy(i,j))*0.5*(f(i,j)+f(i-1,j))  &
     *0.25*(rv(i-1,k,j+1)+rv(i,k,j+1)+rv(i-1,k,j)+rv(i,k,j))                     &
     - 0.5*(e(i,j)+e(i-1,j))*0.5*(cosa(i,j)+cosa(i-1,j))                          &
     *0.25*(rw(i-1,k+1,j)+rw(i-1,k,j)+rw(i,k+1,j)+rw(i,k,j))
\end{lstlisting}
$f$ and $e$ are averaged to the $u$ point by two-point means, $V$ by a four-point mean of the neighbouring $v$
points, and $W$ by a four-point mean over the two adjacent columns and the two levels bounding the half level. For $W$
at a full level, the half-level values of $U$ and $V$ are interpolated vertically with the weights \code{fzm} and
\code{fzp}.

\begin{portnote}
In the source, the averages are written as \code{0.25*(a+b+c+d)} and \code{0.5*(a+b)}: the sums are formed left to
right, and only then multiplied. A port must keep exactly that form. \code{0.25*a+0.25*b+...} or a reordered sum would
round differently. The GPU kernels K-COR-U, -V, -W copy the statements verbatim, and the tool \code{arith_guard}
checks that no expression was rewritten (\cref{ch:repro}).
\end{portnote}

\section{Curvature}

On a sphere of radius $r_e$ (\code{reradius} $= 1/r_e$), advection of momentum along curved coordinate lines produces
apparent forces. In WRF's form they are
\begin{align}
  F_{U,\mathrm{curv}} &= \frac{m_x}{m_y}\,(v\times\nabla m)\,V \;-\; \frac{u\,W}{r_e} + \dots, \notag\\
  F_{V,\mathrm{curv}} &= -\frac{m_y}{m_x}\,(v\times\nabla m)\,U \;-\; \frac{v\,W}{r_e} + \dots,\\
  F_{W,\mathrm{curv}} &= \frac{u\,U + (m_x/m_y)\,v\,V}{r_e}, \notag
\end{align}
where
\begin{equation}
  (v\times\nabla m) = u\,\frac{\partial m}{\partial y} - v\,\frac{\partial m}{\partial x}
\end{equation}
is evaluated at mass points (\code{vxgm} in the code). On a regular latitude--longitude grid an equivalent form in
$\tan\varphi$ is used instead. \code{curvature} computes \code{vxgm} first, then copies its edge values outward so the
averages at the domain edges have neighbours, and only then forms the three tendencies.

\begin{portnote}
\code{curvature} is a small example of an order constraint that a GPU port must respect:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the interior of \code{vxgm};
  \item its edge copies, first in $x$, then in $y$;
  \item the three tendencies, which read the copied edges.
\end{enumerate}
These are separate kernels launched in that order (K-CURV-1 \dots{} K-CURV-6). Fusing steps 1 and 3 would let a thread
read an edge value before another thread had written it.
\end{portnote}

\section{Sizes of the terms}

At the latitude of the case (34\textdegree{}N), $f \approx 8.2\times10^{-5}$~s$^{-1}$, so the Coriolis acceleration of
a 10~m/s wind is below $10^{-3}$~m/s$^2$. Over the case's 17 hours the Coriolis terms still turn the large-scale flow
substantially, but on the time scale of the fire's convective column they are small. The curvature terms are smaller
still on a regional domain. Both are nevertheless computed every RK stage, and both must be bitwise identical on the
GPU, because their effect on the last bits propagates like any other.
