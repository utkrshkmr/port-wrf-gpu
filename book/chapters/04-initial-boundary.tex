\chapter{Initial and boundary conditions}\label{ch:icbc}

\code{real.exe} turns the horizontally interpolated analyses of metgrid into a state that the model's equations
accept. That state must:
\begin{itemize}
  \item use the model's vertical coordinate;
  \item be in discrete hydrostatic balance with the model's own vertical operators;
  \item hold the dry air mass, not the total, as its column mass.
\end{itemize}
This chapter follows \src{dyn_em/module_initialize_real.F}{init_domain_rk} and the boundary assembly of
\src{main/real_em.F}{assemble_output}. The notation is that of \cref{ch:eq}; a bar marks the base state, a prime the
perturbation from it.

\section{The base state}

WRF writes most variables as a time-independent base state plus a perturbation. The base state is a hydrostatically
balanced atmosphere at rest that depends only on terrain height. Keeping it separate reduces truncation errors in the
pressure gradient over steep terrain.

The base-state temperature is a function of pressure alone. With the constants $p_{00}$ (\SI{1e5}{Pa}), $T_{00}$
(the sea-level temperature), $A$ (the lapse in temperature per $e$-fold of pressure), the isothermal floor
$T_{\mathrm{iso}}$, and a stratospheric profile above $p_{\mathrm{strat}}$:
\begin{equation}
  \bar T(\bar p) =
  \begin{cases}
    \max\bigl(T_{\mathrm{iso}},\; T_{00} + A \ln(\bar p/p_{00})\bigr), & \bar p \ge p_{\mathrm{strat}},\\
    T_{\mathrm{iso}} + A_{\mathrm{strat}} \ln(\bar p/p_{\mathrm{strat}}), & \bar p < p_{\mathrm{strat}}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:base-T}
\end{equation}
Integrating the hydrostatic equation $\mathrm{d}\phi = -R_d \bar T\, \mathrm{d}\ln \bar p$ from sea level, with
$\bar T = T_{00} + A\ln(\bar p/p_{00})$, gives a quadratic in $\ln(\bar p/p_{00})$. Its root at the terrain height
$z_s$ is the base-state surface pressure
\begin{equation}
  \bar p_s = p_{00}\exp\!\left(-\frac{T_{00}}{A} + \sqrt{\left(\frac{T_{00}}{A}\right)^{2} - \frac{2 g z_s}{A R_d}}\right),
  \label{eq:base-ps}
\end{equation}
as coded in \src{dyn_em/module_initialize_real.F}{init_domain_rk}. The base dry column mass is
$\bar\mu_d = \bar p_s - p_t$, where $p_t$ is the pressure at the model top.

On every half level $k$, the base pressure follows from the hybrid coordinate of \cref{ch:eq}:
\begin{equation}
  \bar p_k = c_{3h,k}\,(\bar p_s - p_t) + c_{4h,k} + p_t ,
\end{equation}
and the base inverse density from the equation of state,
\begin{equation}
  \bar\alpha_k = \frac{R_d}{p_{1000}}\;\bar\theta_k \left(\frac{\bar p_k}{p_{1000}}\right)^{-c_v/c_p},
  \qquad \bar\theta_k = \bar T(\bar p_k)\left(\frac{p_{00}}{\bar p_k}\right)^{R_d/c_p}.
\end{equation}

\section{Hydrostatic balance in the model's own discretization}

The geopotential $\phi = gz$ lives on full levels (the faces between half levels), and the model updates it with its
own discrete operators. If the initial $\phi$ were taken directly from the analysis, the first model steps would see
a spurious vertical pressure-gradient force and launch acoustic and gravity waves. \code{real.exe} therefore
integrates the geopotential upward with the same discrete relation the model uses (\cref{ch:eq},
\cref{eq:hydro-diag}).

For the base state, with the default \code{hypsometric_opt = 2}, the integration uses the logarithm of the full-level
base pressures $\bar p_{f,k}$:
\begin{equation}
  \bar\phi_{k} = \bar\phi_{k-1} + \bar\alpha_{k-1}\,\bar p_{k-1}\,\ln\frac{\bar p_{f,k-1}}{\bar p_{f,k}},
  \qquad \bar\phi_1 = g z_s .
  \label{eq:phb-hyps2}
\end{equation}
With \code{hypsometric_opt = 1} it is the plain finite difference
$\bar\phi_{k} = \bar\phi_{k-1} - \Delta\eta_{k-1}\,(c_{1h}\bar\mu_d + c_{2h})\,\bar\alpha_{k-1}$.

The perturbation geopotential satisfies the perturbation form of the same relation:
\begin{equation}
  \phi'_{k} = \phi'_{k-1} - \Delta\eta_{k-1}\Bigl[\bigl(c_{1h}(\bar\mu_d+\mu'_d)+c_{2h}\bigr)\alpha'_{k-1}
             + c_{1h}\,\mu'_d\,\bar\alpha_{k-1}\Bigr],
  \label{eq:php-pert}
\end{equation}
which the code evaluates column by column, from the surface up.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{Building the dry hydrostatic column in \code{init_domain_rk} (one horizontal point).}
\label{alg:column}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require terrain height $z_s$, dry surface pressure $p_{ds}$, interpolated $\theta$ and $q_v$ on half levels
\State $\bar p_s \gets$ \cref{eq:base-ps}; \quad $\bar\mu_d \gets \bar p_s - p_t$
\For{$k = 1, \dots, n_z - 1$}
  \State $\bar p_k \gets c_{3h,k}(\bar p_s - p_t) + c_{4h,k} + p_t$; \quad $\bar T \gets$ \cref{eq:base-T}
  \State $\bar\theta_k$, $\bar\alpha_k$ from $\bar T$ and $\bar p_k$
\EndFor
\State $\bar\phi_1 \gets g z_s$
\For{$k = 2, \dots, n_z$} \Comment{upward, in this order}
  \State $\bar\phi_k \gets$ \cref{eq:phb-hyps2}
\EndFor
\State $\mu'_d \gets (p_{ds} - p_t) - \bar\mu_d$
\For{$k = 1, \dots, n_z - 1$}
  \State $p_k \gets c_{3h,k}(\bar\mu_d + \mu'_d) + c_{4h,k} + p_t$ \Comment{dry hydrostatic pressure}
  \State $\alpha_k \gets$ equation of state with $\theta_k$, $q_{v,k}$ and $p_k$; \quad $\alpha'_k \gets \alpha_k - \bar\alpha_k$
\EndFor
\State $\phi'_1 \gets 0$
\For{$k = 2, \dots, n_z$}
  \State $\phi'_k \gets$ \cref{eq:php-pert}
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Dry and total pressure}

The ARW equations use the mass of dry air as the column mass (\cref{ch:eq}). The analyses give the total surface
pressure, which includes the weight of water vapor. \code{real.exe} computes the dry surface pressure by integrating
the moisture of the interpolated column and subtracting its weight. Its routines \code{integ_moist},
\code{p_dts} and \code{p_dhs} in \code{module_initialize_real.F} do this. The perturbation column mass is the
difference between the dry column mass and the base:
\begin{equation}
  \mu'_d = (p_{ds} - p_t) - \bar\mu_d .
\end{equation}
With moist potential temperature (\code{use_theta_m = 1}), the prognostic temperature variable is
$\theta_m = \theta\,(1 + (R_v/R_d)\,q_v)$, and the initial state stores $\theta_m - T_0$ with $T_0 = 300$~K.

\section{Vertical interpolation}

metgrid leaves each field on its source levels, usually isobaric surfaces plus the surface. \code{real.exe}
interpolates each column to the model's half levels (and $w$ and $\phi$ to full levels) with
\src{dyn_em/module_initialize_real.F}{vert_interp}. Namelist options of the \code{&domains} group control it:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the vertical variable of the interpolation: pressure or its logarithm (\code{interp_type});
  \item the order of the Lagrange polynomial (\code{lagrange_order});
  \item extrapolation below the lowest source level and the temperature lapse used there (\code{extrap_type},
    \code{t_extrap_type});
  \item whether the surface value is used as a source level, and how many model levels near the ground are forced
    to use it (\code{use_surface}, \code{force_sfc_in_vinterp}).
\end{itemize}
The model levels themselves come from \src{dyn_em/module_initialize_real.F}{compute_eta}: from user-given $\eta$
values, or automatically from a stretching rule with a bottom layer thickness and a maximum layer thickness.

\section{Soil and surface}

The land model needs the soil temperature and moisture on its own layers: four Noah layers, 0--10, 10--40, 40--100
and 100--200~cm, in this case. \code{real.exe} interpolates them in the vertical from the source model's soil layers.
It also checks that land and water masks agree between the static data (geogrid) and the analyses, and fills
inconsistent points from neighbours. It sets the skin and sea-surface temperatures, snow, and the vegetation
fraction for the start date from the monthly climatologies of geogrid.

\section{The lateral boundary file}

For the outer domain, \code{real.exe} repeats the work for every analysis time $t_n$, $n = 0, 1, \dots$, at the
interval $\Delta t_b$ (\code{interval_seconds}). It then writes the boundary file \code{wrfbdy_d01}. For each
prognostic variable it stores two things on the four sides of the domain (\code{_bxs}, \code{_bxe}, \code{_bys},
\code{_bye}), over a strip of \code{spec_bdy_width} points (five by default):
\begin{itemize}
  \item the value at $t_n$, \emph{coupled} with the column mass and the map factor as the model's prognostic variables
    are (for example $\mu_d u/m_y$): \src{dyn_em/module_big_step_utilities_em.F}{couple}, then \code{stuff_bdy};
  \item its tendency $(\psi_{n+1} - \psi_n)/\Delta t_b$, in the arrays \code{_btxs} \dots: \code{stuff_bdytend}
    in \code{share/module_bc.F}.
\end{itemize}
During the forecast, the model evaluates the boundary value at time $t \in [t_n, t_{n+1}]$ linearly,
\begin{equation}
  \psi_b(t) = \psi_b(t_n) + (t - t_n)\,\left.\pd{\psi}{t}\right|_b ,
\end{equation}
and uses it in the specified and relaxation zones (\cref{ch:bdy}). Nested domains get their boundary values from
their parent during the run, not from a file.

\begin{portnote}
None of this runs on the GPU: \code{real.exe} is a CPU program. The GPU run reads the same \code{wrfinput} and
\code{wrfbdy} files as the CPU reference, and the port checks their checksums before every run. The boundary arrays
are uploaded to the device each time \code{wrf.exe} reads a new boundary time (sync point S5 in \cref{ch:port}).
\end{portnote}
