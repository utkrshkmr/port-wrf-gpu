\chapter{Cumulus and the grey zone}\label{ch:cu}

This chapter gives an overview only: the case runs without a cumulus scheme, so none is ported.

\section{What a cumulus scheme does}

Deep convective clouds are a few kilometres wide. On grids of 10~km or more they are invisible to the dynamics, yet
they carry heat, moisture and momentum vertically and produce much of the precipitation. A cumulus parameterization:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item decides whether a column would convect (a \emph{trigger}, usually based on the buoyancy of a lifted parcel);
  \item computes the mass flux of updrafts and downdrafts from a \emph{closure}, typically the removal of the
    column's convective available potential energy (CAPE) over a time scale;
  \item applies the resulting heating, drying and precipitation as tendencies.
\end{enumerate}
WRF's \code{cumulus_driver} offers several schemes, among them:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Kain--Fritsch (\code{cu_physics = 1}): an entraining--detraining plume with a CAPE closure;
  \item Grell--Freitas (3): an ensemble of closures, scale-aware;
  \item the new Tiedtke (16): a mass-flux scheme from the ECMWF tradition, shared with MPAS
    (\code{physics_mmm/cu_ntiedtke.F90}).
\end{itemize}

\section{The grey zone}

Between about 1 and 10~km, convection is partly resolved: the model makes convective cells, but too large and too
late. A conventional scheme there would convect twice. Scale-aware schemes reduce their mass flux as the grid spacing
shrinks. At 900~m (d01) and 100~m (d02), the case is below the grey zone, so it uses no cumulus scheme at all and lets
the dynamics and the microphysics make the clouds. That includes the pyro-convective column above the fire.

\section{For the port}

A cumulus scheme is column physics like WSM6, and the methods of \cref{ch:colphys} apply. The startup check of the GPU
build rejects \code{cu_physics} $\ne 0$ (test T-GATE). A later phase can add a scheme for coarser outer domains
(\code{roadmap/plan-4.6/stage-8-coverage.md}, phase S8-18).
