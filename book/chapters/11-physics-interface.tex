\chapter{The physics interface}\label{ch:phys}

Physics schemes compute the effects of processes that the dynamics does not resolve:
\begin{itemize}
  \item radiation;
  \item exchanges with the surface;
  \item boundary-layer mixing;
  \item condensation and precipitation;
  \item convection;
  \item fire.
\end{itemize}
They see the atmosphere as physical variables (temperature, pressure, density, height) on columns, and return tendencies
or adjusted states. This chapter describes how WRF converts between the dynamics' variables and the physics'
variables, when each scheme is called, and how its results are coupled back.

\section{When physics runs}

All physics except microphysics runs once per large time step, inside the first Runge--Kutta stage, before the slow
tendencies of that stage are formed (\cref{alg:solve-em}). In \src{dyn_em/module_first_rk_step_part1.F}{first_rk_step_part1}
the order is:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item zero the physics tendencies (\code{init_zero_tendency}) and prepare the physics variables (\code{phy_prep});
  \item radiation (\code{radiation_driver}), on radiation steps only;
  \item surface layer, land surface and surface diagnostics (\code{surface_driver});
  \item boundary layer (\code{pbl_driver});
  \item cumulus and shallow convection (\code{cumulus_driver}, \code{shallowcu_driver}), if selected;
  \item the fire model on its domain (\code{fire_driver_em_step}).
\end{enumerate}
Then \src{dyn_em/module_first_rk_step_part2.F}{first_rk_step_part2}:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item couples the physics tendencies with the column mass (\code{calculate_phy_tend});
  \item computes the diffusion of \cref{ch:turb};
  \item adds the physics tendencies to the dynamics tendencies (\code{update_phy_ten}).
\end{enumerate}
Those tendencies then act in all three RK stages.

Microphysics is the exception. It adjusts the state, not the tendency, and runs once after the third RK stage, on the
updated state (\code{microphysics_driver}, wrapped by \code{moist_physics_prep_em} and \code{moist_physics_finish_em}).
That way saturation adjustment sees the final state of the step.

\paragraph{Physics time steps.} Some schemes are expensive and run less often than every step.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Radiation runs every \code{radt} minutes: every minute in the case, so every 20 steps of d01 and every 180 of
    d02. Between calls its heating rates (\code{RTHRATEN}) are kept and reapplied every step.
  \item The boundary layer and surface run every step (\code{bldt = 0}).
\end{itemize}
The decision is made on the host from the step count, and the GPU follows the same branch.

\section{From dynamics to physics variables}

The dynamics carries $\theta_m - T_0$, $p'$, $\alpha_d$ and $\phi'$. \src{dyn_em/module_big_step_utilities_em.F}{phy_prep}
computes, at every point,
\begin{align}
  \theta &= \frac{T_0 + \theta'_m}{1 + (R_v/R_d)\,q_v}, & p &= \bar p + p', &
  \pi &= \Bigl(\frac{p}{p_{1000}}\Bigr)^{R_d/c_p}, \\
  T &= \theta\,\pi, & \rho &= \frac{1 + q_v}{\alpha_d}, &
  z &= \frac{\bar\phi + \phi'}{g},
\end{align}
plus the layer thicknesses \code{dz8w}, the pressures at full levels \code{p8w} and the temperatures at full levels
\code{t8w}. The surface pressure at full level 1 is extrapolated with
$p_{8w,1} = \exp\bigl(w_1 \ln p_1 + w_2 \ln p_2\bigr)$, with interpolation weights $w_1$, $w_2$. The hydrostatic
pressure at full levels is integrated downward from the top, adding the weight of each layer's water species.

\begin{portnote}
Two lines of \code{phy_prep} show the reproducibility rules of \cref{ch:repro} at work.
\begin{itemize}
  \item The Exner function is \code{pi_phy = rp_pow((p_phy/p1000mb), rcp)}. The original \code{**rcp} would call the
    compiler's power function, which differs between host and device.
  \item The downward integration of the hydrostatic pressure is a recurrence in $k$. On the GPU it runs as one thread
    per column (Template C, kernel K-PPR-6), with the species summed in the source's order.
\end{itemize}
\end{portnote}

\section{Physics tendencies and coupling}

Schemes return tendencies of the uncoupled variables:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{RTHRATEN}: potential temperature tendency from radiation;
  \item \code{RUBLTEN}, \code{RVBLTEN}, \code{RTHBLTEN}, \code{RQVBLTEN}: tendencies from the boundary layer;
  \item \code{RTHFRTEN}, \code{RQVFRTEN}: tendencies from the fire (\cref{ch:fire}).
\end{itemize}
The dynamics needs them as tendencies of the coupled variables. \src{dyn_em/module_em.F}{calculate_phy_tend}
multiplies each by the column mass of its level, $c_1\mu + c_2$. Then \src{phys/module_physics_addtendc.F}{update_phy_ten}
adds them to the tendency arrays of the dynamics:
\begin{itemize}
  \item scalars directly;
  \item $u$ and $v$ after averaging from mass points to their own points (\code{add_a2c_u}, \code{add_a2c_v}).
\end{itemize}
Before the next physics call, \code{phy_prep_part2} divides the stored radiation tendency by the column mass again.
This keeps it in uncoupled form while the column mass changes.

\section{Wrappers and column physics}

Most schemes are written for one $j$ row at a time: their core routines take arrays dimensioned \code{(its:ite,
kts:kte)}, and the driver loops over $j$. A WRF driver (\code{module_*_driver.F}) selects the scheme. For schemes
shared with MPAS (\code{phys/physics_mmm/}), a thin wrapper:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item copies the three-dimensional WRF arrays into two-dimensional slabs;
  \item calls the scheme;
  \item copies the results back.
\end{enumerate}
For example, the wrapper of WSM6 converts $\theta$ to $T$ by multiplying by $\pi$ on the way in and divides by $\pi$
on the way out.

\begin{portnote}
The slab structure is the main obstacle for GPUs. A slab routine works on many columns at once with loops over $i$
inside loops over $k$, and it allocates its temporaries on every call. The port turns each scheme into
\emph{column physics} (\cref{ch:colphys}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item one GPU thread per column;
  \item the same core routine, called with \code{its = ite = 1};
  \item fixed-size private temporaries;
  \item every callee compiled for the device.
\end{itemize}
Each column performs the same operations in the same order as on the CPU, so the result is identical.
\end{portnote}
