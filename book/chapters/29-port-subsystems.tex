\section{Dynamics}

\subsection{Runge--Kutta preparation}

At the start of each RK stage, \code{rk_step_prep} computes the coupled velocities, the vertical mass flux $\omega$,
the moisture coefficients and the inverse density (\cref{alg:solve-em}). Most of it is pointwise (Template A). Two
routines are not:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{calc_ww_cp} integrates the column divergence from the surface upward to obtain $\omega$: a recurrence in
    $k$. It becomes a column kernel (Template C). The source zeroes $\omega$ over \code{its..ite} but integrates over
    \code{its..itf}; the column kernel runs over the union and guards each statement group with its own range.
  \item \code{calc_cq} sums the moisture species to form the moisture coefficient. The sum stays a sequential loop in
    the thread, over the species in the source's order.
\end{itemize}
The physical boundary conditions (\code{set_physical_bc2d}, \code{_3d}) are boundary strips (Template G): an $x$ phase
kernel and a $y$ phase kernel, in that order, because the $y$ copies read corners the $x$ copies wrote.

\subsection{Large-step tendencies}

\paragraph{Advection.} \code{advect_u}, \code{advect_v}, \code{advect_w} and \code{advect_scalar} each become four
kernel families: Y1 and Y2 for the $y$-fluxes (Template B), X for the $x$-fluxes, and Z for the vertical fluxes,
a column with the zero fluxes at the bottom and the lid set inside the kernel. The branches by row (5th order in the
interior, 3rd and 2nd order next to boundaries) are kept exactly: the same row gets the same formula.

\paragraph{Quirks kept.} The port reproduces what the source does, including what may be unintended:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{rhs_ph} has no 4th-order $x$-advection at \code{ids+2} and \code{ide-3} under specified or nested
    boundaries, because that branch is guarded by \code{open_xs}/\code{open_xe} only;
  \item \code{w_damp} activates on \code{w_beta} but offsets by \code{w_crit_cfl};
  \item \code{add_a2c_v} runs its $k$ loop to \code{kte}, where its sibling \code{add_a2c_u} stops at \code{ktf = min(kte,kde-1)}.
\end{itemize}
A bitwise port is not the place to fix them. Each is recorded, so that a later change can be made deliberately and
in both builds.

\paragraph{Pressure gradient and buoyancy.} \code{horizontal_pressure_gradient} uses a slab of pressure differences
per row, which becomes a private column (Template C). \code{pg_buoy_w} writes the top level from the original
\code{cqw(kde-1)} and then converts \code{cqw} in place, so it becomes two kernels in that order.

\paragraph{Reductions.} \code{w_damp} also reports the largest vertical and horizontal Courant numbers. A maximum of
floating-point numbers is exact in any order, so it is computed with a parallel \code{reduction(max:)}. The location of
the maximum and the warning messages are not computed on the device: when the debug level asks for them, a host pass
recomputes them from the same data.

\subsection{The acoustic loop}

Each d02 step contains seven acoustic sub-steps (one, two and four in the three RK stages), and each sub-step calls
about ten routines (\cref{alg:small-step}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{advance_uv}: a column kernel per velocity component, with the pressure-gradient slab as a private
    column and two statement groups with different $i$ ranges;
  \item \code{advance_mu_t}: the column-integrated divergence, an upward recurrence for $\omega$, and the
    potential-temperature update;
  \item \code{advance_w}: one column kernel containing the whole implicit solve (\cref{alg:tridiag}): the right-hand
    side, the forward and backward sweeps of the tridiagonal system with the coefficients of \code{calc_coef_w}, the
    Rayleigh damping at the top, and the geopotential update;
  \item the boundary updates of the nest (\code{spec_bdyupdate}, \code{spec_bdyupdate_ph}): boundary strips.
\end{itemize}
In \code{advance_w}, the damping weight is $\sin^2$ written as \code{rp_sin(x)*rp_sin(x)}, two calls and a product,
as in the source, and $\pi$ is \code{4.*rp_atan(1.)} in default precision, computed on the host and passed into the
kernel. Writing $\sin^2$ any other way would round differently.

The acoustic loop launches about 150 kernels per d02 step, each running a few microseconds on the dev case. It is
where launch overhead matters most (\cref{ch:fusion}).

\subsection{Scalar transport}

Moisture species and TKE are advected in every RK stage, with the positive-definite version
\code{advect_scalar_pd} in the last stage. Its limiter is the reason for Template L: the cell loop that scales
outflow fluxes becomes a cell kernel and three face kernels (\cref{ch:port}). The eight temporary 3D arrays of the
routine, which the source allocates on the stack at every call, become work arrays. The scalar updates
(\code{rk_update_scalar}) are pointwise.

\subsection{End of step}

\code{calc_p_rho_phi} computes the full pressure from the equation of state. The source calls a vector power routine
of WRF's MASSV library (\code{vspow}); the kernel inlines the same operation as \code{rp_pow}, which the CPU-REF
version of \code{vspow} also calls, so both builds evaluate the identical function.

\subsection{Turbulence}

The deformation tensor (\code{cal_deform_and_div}) is about forty short loop nests, each its own kernel in the source
order; horizontal scalar diffusion is eleven. The TKE routines evaluate $e^{3/2}$, $(\Delta x\Delta y\Delta z)^{1/3}$
and similar powers with \code{rp_pow}. This part of the step is many small pointwise kernels over the whole domain, a
natural target for fusion once the port is complete (\cref{ch:fusion}).

\section{Physics}

\subsection{Glue}

\code{phy_prep} converts the dynamics' variables to the physics' (\cref{ch:phys}): mostly pointwise, except the
hydrostatic pressure at full levels, which is integrated downward from the model top, with a sum over the moisture
species at each level. It becomes a column kernel with the species sum sequential in the source's order. The coupling
of the radiation and boundary-layer tendencies with the dry-air mass (\code{calculate_phy_tend}) is pointwise.

\subsection{WSM6}

WSM6 is the first column-physics scheme of the port (Template CP). Its core, \code{mp_wsm6_run}, has about 78
automatic arrays of shape \code{(its:ite,kts:kte)}, ten of them with a third dimension, and its sedimentation routines
about 27 column vectors; all become fixed-size. Whole-array statements such as \code{precip(:) = 0.0} are rewritten to
the active extent, because the fixed arrays are longer than the column. The statement functions (\code{cpmcal},
\code{xlcal}, \code{diffus}, \dots) stay if the compiler accepts them in device code, or become module functions with
the identical expression. The surface accumulators (rain, snow, graupel) are copied per column into one-element
arrays, so that the accumulation happens inside \code{mp_wsm6_run} as in the source.

The effective-radius calculation returns at once in this configuration (\code{has_reqc = 0}), so the GPU wrapper skips
its call: the values it would copy are unchanged.

\subsection{Surface}

\paragraph{sfclayrev.} Only the lowest level is used, so each thread gathers one point. The secant iteration
\code{zolri} (\cref{alg:zolri}) could return an undefined value; the port defines it in both builds, and counts the
occurrences: T-ZOLRI must find none in the reference run, which proves that the fix changes nothing in this case.

\paragraph{Noah.} One thread runs the whole land-surface model for its point: \code{SFLX} and its tree of about thirty
routines (\cref{alg:noah}). The changes are about data, not arithmetic:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the point indices \code{iloc}, \code{jloc}, which Noah keeps in module variables, become arguments, since a
    module variable is shared by all threads;
  \item the land-use and soil type names, character strings of 256 characters, become integer codes;
  \item locals that Fortran saves implicitly because they are initialized in their declaration become
    \code{PARAMETER}s;
  \item the parameter tables are uploaded once.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{YSU}

YSU (d01 only) follows Template CP. Its wrapper copies thirteen arrays of the urban model even when the urban model is
off, from arrays allocated with size one: an out-of-bounds read on the CPU. The port guards the copies by the urban
flag in both builds; the values were never used, so nothing changes. The PBL-height search \code{get_pblh} is a
\code{DO WHILE} loop without a bound, kept as it is; the column harness checks that it terminates.

\subsection{Radiation}

Radiation runs every step for accumulations and cloud fractions, and fully once a minute (every 20 d01 steps and every
180 d02 steps).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Ozone.} \code{ozn_p_int} follows Template R, so that the bracket search of each column starts where
    the source's did.
  \item \textbf{Tables.} RRTMG's initialization rebuilt its absorption tables at every radiation call; the port calls
    it once at initialization and uploads the tables. The tables were declared with \code{EQUIVALENCE}, which device
    code does not support; each pair becomes one 1D array with explicit index arithmetic, in the same storage order.
  \item \textbf{Batching.} An RRTMG column needs about 1.1~MB of temporary storage (109 layers, including the 50
    layers added above the model top, times 140 $g$-points). The columns are processed in batches of 4096
    (\code{WRF_RRTMG_BATCH}), with the per-column temporaries hoisted into batch work arrays of about 4.5~GB.
  \item \textbf{Random numbers.} The McICA cloud sampling draws from the KISS generator, seeded from the pressures.
    Its integer arithmetic relies on 32-bit wraparound; T-KISS checks that host and device give the same streams.
  \item \textbf{Dudhia shortwave.} One thread per column, with the night columns leaving early (\cref{ch:warp}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Fire}

\paragraph{Data.} WRF-Fire keeps its fuel parameters in pointer components of a derived type (\texttt{fp\%r\_0},
\dots). The port passes them to the kernels as explicit arrays. The flags that \code{set_flags} sets at every fire
call, always to the same values, are set once per domain at initialization. The statistics routines checked for NaN
by summing floating-point values; they now count NaNs with an integer reduction.

\paragraph{Ghost cells.} The fuel calculation reads the outermost fire rows and columns, which are never written: on
the CPU they keep the zeros of their allocation. The device copy is made by \code{enter data copyin} from the host
array, so it holds the same zeros. T-FIRE-GHOST checks it.

\paragraph{Level set.} \code{tend_ls} becomes one kernel over the fire nodes, with the upwinded differences, the
WENO/ENO choice by node, the call of \code{fire_ros} (Template D), the artificial viscosity, and a maximum reduction for
the stable time step. \code{continue_at_boundary} becomes three kernels in the source order: $j$ strips, $i$ strips,
corners. \code{interpolate_2d} gives each coarse cell to one thread, which writes its $4 \times 4$ fine nodes. This is
safe only because the refinement ratio is even, so that no two coarse cells write the same node; the startup check
rejects odd ratios.

\paragraph{Fuel and fluxes.} \code{fuel_left} gives each fire cell to one thread, which evaluates its four sub-cells
in the source's order. \code{sum_2d_cells} sums the fire-grid fluxes over each atmospheric cell: one thread per
atmospheric cell, the inner loops over the fine cells sequential in the source's order. A parallel sum here would add
in a different order and change the heat that drives the plume.

\paragraph{Fire windows.} The fire's sensitivity (\cref{ch:fire}) makes its tests the strictest of the port:
\begin{itemize}
  \item T-FIRE-IGN runs from 02:00 to 02:35, through the whole ignition period, and compares every fire array at every
    step;
  \item T-FIRE-WIN runs from 02:20 to 03:00 and compares the burned cells at each history time.
\end{itemize}

\section{Nest forcing}

Once per d01 step, the parent forces the nest's lateral boundaries (\cref{ch:bdy}). The forcing couples both domains'
fields with the dry-air mass, interpolates parent values to the nest boundary on the host, and uncouples again. The
port moves the coupling to the device. It cannot be skipped: uncoupling multiplies by a stored reciprocal, so
coupling followed by uncoupling does not return the original bits ($x\,m\,(1/m) \ne x$ in general), and CPU-REF does
both.

The interpolation stays on the host, and only what it reads and writes moves:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the parent rows that the interpolation reads, a slab of the parent's fields;
  \item the six boundary rows and columns of the nest's own fields, which the boundary interpolation also reads;
  \item back to the device: the nest's boundary arrays and \code{o3rad}, the one field the forcing writes in full.
\end{itemize}
T-SLAB proves that these lists are complete: it fills everything outside them on the host with signaling NaNs before
the forcing, and the results must still equal CPU-REF.

\section{Where the port stands}

At the time of writing, the port has finished its ground work (Phase~0, \code{port/RESULTS.md}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item the reproducible math library, correctly rounded on every single-precision input of each function;
  \item the substitutions of 646 intrinsic calls and 265 powers, certified bitwise on two smoke cases;
  \item the tracer, routes, pool, comparison tools and gates.
\end{itemize}
A first code-only run wrote kernels for 19 of the 44 work packages of Phases 1 to 3. They are now being landed,
compiled and tested on the A100 workstation (Stage~0 of the plan). The device results of every test named in this
chapter are \tbr.
